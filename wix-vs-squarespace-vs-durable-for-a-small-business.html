Wix vs Squarespace vs Durable for a Small Service Business

The short answer

If you want the widest choice and are willing to spend a weekend learning it, Wix. If you want something that looks designed without you making design decisions, Squarespace. If you want a site live today and are happy to trade some control for speed, Durable.

For most one-person service businesses — a plumber, a cleaner, a mobile hairdresser — the honest answer is the cheapest of the three you will actually finish. An unfinished Squarespace site is worth less than a finished Durable one.

Wix: the one with everything

Wix has been around since 2006 and it shows. There are templates for every trade, an app market for anything you might want to bolt on — bookings, memberships, email marketing, a shop — and a free tier you can genuinely start on.

The cost of that flexibility is decisions. You will spend your first hour choosing a template, not building a site. The editor gives you near-total control, which means it also lets you make a mess. And the free plan puts Wix's branding on your pages and restricts commercial use, so it is a testing ground, not a home.

Choose Wix if: you want a specific feature set, you are willing to learn an interface, or you might later want to sell products or take bookings.

Squarespace: the one that looks good by default

Squarespace's advantage is restraint. Fewer templates, each better than the average Wix one, and typography and spacing that hold up without you adjusting anything. If you have no eye for design and no interest in developing one, this matters more than a feature list.

The trade is customisation. Where Wix lets you move anything, Squarespace mostly asks you to accept its layout. For a five-page service site that is usually a feature, not a limitation. There is no permanent free plan, only a trial, so you are committing to a paid tier reasonably early.

Choose Squarespace if: the site needs to look considered — a salon, a clinic, a photographer, a restaurant — and you do not want to think about design.

Durable: the one that finishes the job

Durable generates a site from a short questionnaire about your business. You answer questions about what you do, where you work and what you charge, and it produces a structured starting point in minutes rather than hours. It is aimed squarely at exactly the businesses this site is written for: solo and small service businesses with no interest in web design.

What you give up is control and range. If your business is unusual, or you need something Durable has not anticipated, you will feel the edges quickly. It is also the least suitable if you plan to grow into e-commerce.

Choose Durable if: you are a trade or a one-person service, you want to be live this week, and you would rather spend your weekend working than choosing fonts.

What none of them will do for you

Whichever you pick, these four things are still yours to get right, and they matter more than the platform:

  • Your own photographs. All three look cheap filled with stock images and expensive filled with real ones.
  • Your prices, or a starting figure. "Contact us" costs you enquiries.
  • Your service area, named. Towns and suburbs, not "the local area".
  • One call to action per page. Call, book, or quote — not all three at once.

The one-line version

Pick the one you will actually finish this month. A finished Durable site beats an abandoned Squarespace one every time, and all three can produce a site that looks better than most $2,000 agency builds.
